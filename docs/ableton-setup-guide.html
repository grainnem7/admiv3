Ableton Live as the Board Sequencer's sound

ADMI stays the brain: the board, the loops, the scenes, the clock and everything on screen. Ableton only makes the sound. This guide is for the facilitator setting up the laptop, not for a programmer.

ADMI's own sound is always one press away: on the Connections tab, Sound from: ADMI. If the MIDI port disappears during a piece, ADMI switches to its own sound by itself and says so on screen.

1. loopMIDI: the cable between Chrome and Ableton

loopMIDI is already installed on this laptop. It makes a "virtual cable": ADMI sends into it from Chrome, and Ableton listens to it.

  1. Open loopMIDI from the Start menu (or the small icon by the clock).
  2. Look in the list for the ADMI port. On this laptop it is called ADMIOutput; that is the one Ableton and the template use, so choose it wherever this guide says ADMI. On a new laptop, type ADMI in the "New port name" box and press +, then restart Ableton so it sees the new port.
  3. Right click the loopMIDI icon by the clock and tick Autostart (start with Windows), so the port is there every time.
  4. Start loopMIDI before Chrome. Chrome only sees the ports that exist when it opens. If you made the port with Chrome already open, close Chrome completely and open it again.

2. Ableton's preferences

Open Ableton Live 12 Suite, then Options → Settings (Ctrl + ,).

Audio

  • Audio Device: the interface or speakers you will use on the night.
  • Buffer Size: 128 samples. Smaller is quicker; 128 keeps the delay from counter to sound small. If you hear crackles, try 256 and check the timing again (section 6).

Link, Tempo and MIDI

Find ADMI under the MIDI Ports list, on the Input line:

  • Track: On. This lets the tracks play ADMI's notes.
  • Remote: On. This lets you map ADMI's controllers to Ableton's controls (the fades, the volume).
  • Sync: Off for now. It is for a later stage, when Ableton will follow ADMI's tempo.

Leave Ableton's own Scale off (the Scale switch in the Set and on each clip). ADMI already sends the right notes in its key; a scale in Ableton would bend them to other notes.

3. The ADMI template Set

Build this once and save it as ADMI template (File → Save Live Set As). Every show starts from it.

Drums

Put a Drum Rack on the channel 10 track. ADMI sends General MIDI drum notes, which match a Drum Rack's default pads: kick 36 (C1), rim 37, snare 38, clap 39, closed hat 42, tom 45, crash 49. Load a kit from the Browser (Drums) and check those pads have sounds.

Fades and Mute: a Utility on every track

ADMI sends its endings and Mute as CC 11 (Expression) on every channel. It also makes the notes softer through a fade, which every instrument hears. Ableton's instruments do not follow CC 11 unless you map it, so:

  1. Drag a Utility (Audio Effects) to the end of each track. (The ADMI template already has them.)
  2. In ADMI, Connections tab, with Sound from on Ableton and the port chosen, find Map in Ableton.
  3. Stop ADMI (during a timed piece, press Stop twice). In Map Mode Ableton learns from any MIDI it hears, notes included.
  4. In Ableton press Ctrl + M (MIDI Map Mode: the screen turns blue).
  5. Click the Utility's Gain on the track ADMI names under "Next to map", then press Send in ADMI. ADMI moves on to the next track by itself; repeat for each.
  6. The last one is Volume: click the Main track's volume fader, then press Send.
  7. Still in Map Mode, in the Mappings list on the left, set Max to 0 dB on every Gain mapping. A Utility's Gain goes up to +35 dB, so without this full Expression would be far too loud.
  8. Press Ctrl + M again to leave, and save the Set. The tracks stay silent until ADMI plays: Play sends full Expression.

Volume: the Main track

ADMI's Volume counter goes out as CC 7 on channel 16, mapped to the Main track's volume in the steps above.

Save the Set as ADMI template once all of this is done.

4. ADMI's Connections tab

  1. Open the Board Sequencer, then the Connections tab.
  2. Under Sound from, press Ableton. Chrome asks to use MIDI devices the first time: allow it.
  3. Under MIDI port, choose ADMI. It says ✓ Sending to ADMI.
  4. Both plays ADMI's sound and Ableton's together: useful for the timing check, or to layer the two.

In Ableton mode the Sound tab says Sound set in Ableton on the controls that only change ADMI's own sound (sound pickers, Loud, Bright, Space, World, Studio mix, the Advanced mix, and the Tone, Reverb and Delay counters). Energy, Groove, Extra notes, Band, Sounds change, and the Fill, Evolve, Tempo and Volume counters still work, because they change the notes or are sent to Ableton.

5. A track per colour (optional)

Two melody colours share channel 1 in the usual setting, so they play the same Ableton instrument. To give each colour its own:

  1. On the Connections tab, under Channels, choose One per colour.
  2. Each colour gets its own channel (from 1 to 9, then 14 to 16) and keeps it, even if colours are moved or added. You can change it in the table there; each colour card on the Sound tab shows MIDI ch and its number.
  3. In Ableton, add a MIDI track for each of those channels (MIDI From ADMI, that channel, Monitor In), with a Utility mapped to CC 11 as above.

Drums, the band and the Extra notes stay on 10, 11, 12 and 13.

5a. ADMI's sounds in Ableton (Instrument Racks)

Every sound you can choose in ADMI has its own sound in Ableton: choose Flute and Ableton plays a flute. ADMI picks a chain in each track's Instrument Rack with CC 30, so a colour's sound, the World, a scene's World and Sounds change all switch Ableton's sound too, instantly. Every rack has the same 33 chains in the same order. A track only needs real instruments in the chains it can play; the others hold a light placeholder. ADMI's Connections tab (Template checklist) says which chains are real on which track. It is long, but done once, one track at a time.

The placeholder: Operator with its default sound (a plain tone). It costs almost nothing, and if you ever hear it you know a chain is missing its real instrument. ADMI never sends a track to a placeholder on purpose.

Changing from the 16-sound racks

If your racks were built with the earlier 16 chains, convert every rack first, before anything else, so no track plays the wrong sound. On each track, but not the drums (channel 10), which keep their four kits:

  1. Rename chain 11 to Cello, 14 to Choir pad, 15 to Electric piano chords and 16 to Bells.
  2. Add chains 17 to 33 from the table below, each holding the placeholder.
  3. Show the Chain select ruler (the "Chain" button above the chain list), select every chain, right click a chain and choose Distribute Ranges Equally.
  4. Check the ranges in the ruler against the table below: chain 4 should start at 12, chain 18 should start at 66 and chain 30 should start at 112. If they don't, drag those chains' edges to match the table; otherwise ADMI would pick the sound next door.

Every track now plays the right chain again, with the new sounds as placeholders. Choir pad, Electric piano chords and Bells still hold their old instruments (the old pad, stab and bell/pluck): give them their new ones as you put the real instruments in, as below.

Building the racks

  1. Melody first, with all 33 real. On the Melody track (channel 1), drag in an Instrument Rack (Instruments), show its Chain list and add the 33 chains above, in this order, named exactly, each with its real instrument. Then show the Chain select ruler, select every chain and choose Distribute Ranges Equally, with no fades, and check the ranges as above (chain 4 should start at 12, chain 18 should start at 66 and chain 30 should start at 112).
  2. Check Melody before building the rest (next section).
  3. Copy it to Bass (channel 2) and Chords (channel 3): select the Rack, Ctrl + C, select the other track's instrument, Ctrl + V. Swap the four World chains for sounds that suit that part.
  4. Trim each track: on Melody, Bass and Chords, replace the instrument in every chain the Template checklist marks Placeholder for that track with the placeholder. The chain stays, with its name.
  5. The band (11, 12), Extra notes (13) and the loop tracks: the same 33 chains, with only the four World chains real and the rest placeholders.
  6. After the Rack on each track, add a Utility named Loud and an Auto Filter named Bright. Space uses the track's Send A. (Macros inside the Rack work too, but these need far less setup.)
  7. Map them: in ADMI, Connections tab, Map in Ableton, choose Sounds and macros. Stop ADMI. In Ableton press Ctrl + M, click the control on the track ADMI names, then press Send in ADMI. It moves on to the next by itself.
    • Chain selector (CC 30): click the Rack's Chain selector. If a macro drives it (the selector is greyed out), click that macro instead.
    • Loud (CC 24): the Loud Utility's Gain. Bright (CC 25): the Bright filter's Freq. Space (CC 26): the track's Send A. Drums, the band and Extra notes get Bright and Space only, for the Tone and Reverb counters.
    • Space for the whole piece (CC 22 on channel 16): the A Reverb return's volume. Energy (CC 21) and Groove (CC 23) are optional. Echo (the Delay counter, CC 27) is optional too.
  8. Loud that plays harder (worth it): wind, brass and string instruments from the Orchestral packs have an Expression or Dynamics control. Still in Ctrl + M, open each of those chains, click that control and press Send for Loud (CC 24) again. Ableton lets one controller move several controls, so Loud then makes a flute or trumpet play harder, not only louder. Give each Expression mapping Min 40% (Max 100%), or the Soft step is far too faint.
  9. Still in Ctrl + M, set each mapping's Min and Max in the Mappings list on the left: Loud Gain Min −18 dB, Max 0 dB; Send A Min −40 dB, Max 0 dB; Return A Min −24 dB, Max 0 dB; Bright Freq Min 600 Hz. Without a Min the lowest step is far too quiet. Leave Chain selectors at 0 to 127.
  10. Drums (ch 10): an Instrument Rack holding four Drum Racks, one per World (Warm, Lo fi, Ambient, Electronic), zones 0 to 7, 8 to 15, 16 to 23, 24 to 31, its Chain selector on CC 30. The drums keep these four zones; they don't use the 33 chains.
  11. Save the Set as ADMI template.

The Melody check (before building the rest)

One Melody rack with all 33 real is the heaviest one track can be. Check it on the laptop you will perform on:

  1. Memory: open Task Manager (Ctrl + Shift + Esc) and note Ableton Live's memory before adding the rack and after.
  2. Load time: save the Set, close Ableton, open the Set again and time it.
  3. Every chain answers: in ADMI, with Sound from Ableton, choose each sound for a melody colour in turn and press ▶ Hear. Each should sound like its name. A plain tone means that chain still holds the placeholder. The melody sounds don't reach every chain: when Bass and Chords are built, do the same with a bass colour and a chords colour.
  4. A full run: play the whole 7-minute piece with the camera and ADMI running, watching Ableton's CPU meter (top right). Aim for under 60% with no crackles or dropouts.

If it is too heavy, choose lighter versions of the heaviest instruments rather than fewer sounds. When Melody passes, build the rest, then do one final full run with the whole Set before relying on it in a show.

If Ableton was restarted, or a track is on the wrong sound, press Send sound choices to Ableton on the Connections tab. ADMI also sends them all on Play.

With one channel per part, two melody colours share channel 1 and so share one Ableton sound: the colour card says so. Choose One per colour to give each its own; then every colour's track needs all 33 chains real.

5b. A fader per loop

ADMI fades a loop in and out by making its notes softer. Many Ableton instruments hardly change volume with that, so a loop would play at full level and then stop. With loops on their own tracks, each loop fades smoothly and has its own fader.

  1. In ADMI, Connections tab, tick A fader per loop in Ableton. Loops 1 to 6 now send their melody, bass and chords on channels 4 to 9 (loop 7 and after share 9). A loop's drums stay on the drum track (10), and fade by how hard they play.
  2. Colours with their own channel then use 1 to 3 and 14 to 15 (ADMI moves them for you).
  3. In Ableton add six MIDI tracks, named Loop 1 to Loop 6: MIDI From the ADMI port, channels 4 to 9, Monitor In. Each needs an instrument (or an Instrument Rack, as in 5a) and a Utility at the end.
  4. Map each loop track's Utility Gain to CC 11 with Map in Ableton → Fades and volume: the loop channels are in its list. Set each mapping's Max to 0 dB.

A loop holds several parts, and on its own track they all play through that track's one instrument. If that matters for a loop, leave this off: the loop then plays on the part tracks with their own sounds, and fades by velocity only.

6. The timing check (aim: under 20 ms)

By ear

  1. Set Ableton's buffer to 128 samples.
  2. In ADMI choose Both and press Play (an empty board is fine).
  3. On the Connections tab press Play test note: ADMI clicks and Ableton plays a note on channel 1 at the same scheduled moment.
  4. One clean hit: good. A double hit (a flam): move Send early by (0 to 40 ms) a few milliseconds at a time until they land together.
  5. Choose Ableton again for the piece.

Measured (loopback)

  1. Connect the audio interface's output back into one of its inputs with a cable.
  2. In Ableton, record an audio track from that input while pressing Play test note a few times in Both mode.
  3. Zoom in on the recording: the gap between ADMI's click and Ableton's note is the difference. Ableton's own extra delay is shown in Settings → Audio as "Overall Latency".

7. Show day checklist

  • loopMIDI open, with the ADMI port, before Chrome.
  • Ableton open with the ADMI template Set, buffer 128, the right audio device.
  • ADMI started with npm run show; Connections tab: Sound from Ableton, port ADMI, "✓ Sending to ADMI".
  • Press Play, then Play test note: it plays one note on each channel in turn (1, 2, 3, 10, 11, 12, 13) and names it on screen. A silent one is a missing track. Extra notes need track 13, the band 11 and 12.
  • The Ready check (Make a piece, step 3) says "MIDI port ADMI connected". It can only see the port, not whether Ableton is listening: that is what the test note is for.
  • A short test ending: Ableton fades with ADMI.
  • A full 7 minute run on this laptop with this Set, at least once before the show (the camera, Chrome and Ableton together use a lot of the laptop).

8. When something goes wrong

  • Ableton freezes or crashes: press ADMI under Sound from. ADMI's own sound comes straight back and the piece carries on.
  • The port disappears (loopMIDI closed, a cable out): ADMI switches to its own sound by itself and shows "Ableton's port has gone, so ADMI is playing its own sound." When the port comes back, ADMI waits: press Switch to Ableton when it suits the music. It never jumps twice in a piece on its own.
  • A note is stuck on in Ableton: press Panic (Connections tab, or in Show). It sends every note off and all sound off on all 16 channels. The piece carries on.
  • Notes stop after the laptop has slept: close Chrome, restart loopMIDI, then open Chrome and ADMI again.
  • No sound from one part: check that track's MIDI From is ADMI with the right channel, and Monitor is In.
  • Ableton doesn't fade at the end: the Utility's Gain is not mapped to CC 11 on that track (section 3). The notes still get softer, but the long ones keep ringing.
