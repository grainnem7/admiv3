Sound and AI Redesign

Your feedback: "I don't see how to adjust the sounds really or adjust the generative stuff — this still seems super unintuitive." A UI/UX designer, a digital-instrument designer and a researcher reviewed the app and studied about 25 similar instruments. They agree on the cause and on most of the cure.

The cause, in one line

The instrument can already do almost everything needed, but the Sound tab is in the order features were built, not the order a musician thinks. The world, two AI processes, the band, a playing mode, then the colours eight screens down, with a mixer of unlabelled 48-pixel sliders at the bottom. And nothing on the board shows what the AI added.

1 · The biggest change

One sound card per colour

Every colour gets a card that answers "what does this colour sound like, and how do I change it?" in one place, at the top of the Sound tab:

  • Its sound, by name ("Soft piano"), with ‹ › to step through 4–6 sounds chosen for the sound world. ▶ Hear plays a short taste. "More sounds…" has the full list.
  • Three simple controls in big steps instead of sliders: Loud, Bright, Space. Volume, tone and reverb or echo are behind those words.
  • Plays: one note / a tune, set per colour. Drums default to a groove, and melody can be one note.
  • Changes over time ✓ lets each colour opt in or out.
  • A "customised" dot with Reset to world: changing the sound world never silently overwrites a colour you chose.

The colour chips above the board also say the sound: ● Red · Melody · Soft piano. Tapping one opens its card.

2 · The whole piece

The overall feel: World, Energy, Space, Groove

Under the colour cards are four controls for the whole instrument:

  • World: Warm, Lo-fi, Ambient or Electronic, with a line saying what it sets ("Blue → upright bass, room: medium").
  • Energy: one control for how much is going on. It turns up the dynamics, brings the band from quiet to full, and makes the drums busier. Tim can play it as one big gesture: a counter slid up the board, or a strip on the iPad.
  • Space: dry to roomy, using the reverb and echo.
  • Groove: Straight, Swung or Lazy.

Scenes store these too, so "Arrival → Build → Open → Home" becomes four Energy levels rather than live re-mixing.

3 · The generative parts

Three helpers, in plain words, with the same buttons

Today, "Fill", "Evolve", "Band", "New idea", "New sound", "Keep", "Hold" and "Magenta / Simple rules" all compete. They become three helpers, named by what you'll hear, each with the same three buttons:

  • Band plays along: Off · Quiet · Full. It says what it's playing ("pad · bass, you have melody and drums").
  • Extra notes (today's Fill) are added around Tim's: Off · A few · Some · Lots. It shows a live count ("◌ 8 now").
  • Sounds change (today's Evolve): Off · Slowly · More. It says what changed ("Red → Rhodes").
  • The same three buttons on each helper: ↻ Try another, ↶ Previous and 📌 Freeze. Keep and Hold become one word, and the iPad uses the same words.
  • Hear the extra notes alone / without them, to judge what the AI is adding.
  • "Magenta or Simple rules" moves to Advanced as "AI engine", with automatic fallback.

A trust problem the instrument designer found: above 20%, "Evolve" changes Tim's notes and rhythms (other tunes, other basslines, busier drums), although the screen says "your notes stay the same". Proposal: Sounds change only ever changes the sound. Changing the patterns becomes a separate, clearly labelled opt-in ("☐ Also vary the patterns").
4 · Seeing it

The board shows who played what

  • A key under the board: ● Tim's counters · ◌ extra notes added by the AI · ◎ a loop · ▬ the band.
  • Extra notes drawn larger, as dotted rings in the colour's swatch that light when they sound, with a Show extra notes switch. Freeze turns them solid.
  • A band strip under the board: "Band: pad · bass", lighting on the beat.
  • When a sound changes, the colour chip reads "Red · now Rhodes" for a moment.

This makes the AI's contribution something you can see and judge, and it meets the "visual feedback for every sound" principle for deaf and hard-of-hearing players.

5 · Loops

Loops you can recognise, and an editor that can't be mistaken for the board

  • A small picture of each loop next to its name, instead of only "2 notes".
  • The edit window shows both, side by side: "On the board now" and "In this loop". It has a Copy the board into this loop button, a ▶ preview, and a different look (dashed border, "Saved loop, not the board" tag). This answers "why does it show two?".
  • Plainer words: "✎" becomes Edit notes, "Replace" becomes Overwrite with board, and in scenes "Go" and "From here" become Jump here now and Rehearse from here.
  • Scenes use the same simple steps (Band Quiet, Extra notes A few, Sounds change Slowly) instead of 0–100% sliders, which also fixes the sliders with no visible track.

6 · The music itself

Musical fixes the instrument designer found in the code

ProblemWhat you hearFix
A wrong chord in ElectronicIts built-in progression loops a diminished chord every 8 beats, which sounds like a mistake.Use a progression without it (I–V–vi–IV), or only use that chord in minor keys.
Chords change too fastFour chords per 8-beat loop: the same 2 bars about 80 times in 7 minutes.Slower chord changes spanning two loops, with a "chords change every: 2 beats / bar / 2 bars" choice.
One square changes the whole tuneMoving a melody counter one square left picks a completely different tune, the opposite of "tolerance over precision".The colour (and scene) picks the tune, and the square only sets when and how high. A near-miss gives the same idea a step later.
Chord counters jumpTim's chord counters jump between voicings, while the band's chords glide.Voice-lead them like the band does.
Evolve changes notesSee section 3.Sound only, unless "vary the patterns" is ticked.
Swing is unevenThe swing setting moves both sixteenths, which smears the hi-hats.Proper eighth-note swing; cap the combined amount.
7 · Tim's hands

On the iPad and the board

  • Energy as one gesture: an iPad strip (replacing Dynamics), and a counter colour that can be an Energy fader on the board. One slide raises the band, the drums and the dynamics together.
  • The same words as the laptop: Extra notes and Sounds change strips, each with its own "↻ Try another" and "📌 Freeze" pads placed under it, so the button sits under the thing it acts on.
  • Later: "Moments" pads, such as Drop (filter sweep), Break (drums only) and Big. Each latches for 1, 2 or 4 bars and lets go by itself, so nothing needs holding.

The order I'd build it in

Round 1
The Sound tab rebuilt: colour sound cards, Overall feel (World, Energy, Space, Groove), the three helpers with the same buttons, Advanced for the rest. The board key and larger AI notes. Loop thumbnails and the new edit window. Plain words throughout. Evolve stops changing notes unless asked.
Round 2
The musical fixes in section 6, with before and after you can hear. Scenes store Energy, Space and Groove.
Round 3
The iPad: Energy strip, matching words and pads, an Energy fader counter. Then Moments, "Hear alone", and a "sometimes" chance per colour.

Nothing here removes a feature. Everything that exists stays reachable; it is regrouped, renamed, and the rarely used parts move into "Advanced".
