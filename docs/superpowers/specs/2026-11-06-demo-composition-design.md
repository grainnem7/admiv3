# The demo piece, composed: "Rising Light"

Owner's brief (2026-11-06): the demo is boring and its instruments sound bad. Lean on pads
more than instruments. Think like a composer and make something brilliant that engages an
audience. Keep using Magenta. It must be **mainly ambient**, pleasant, and that includes
what Tim's own counters play; but it must **keep changing**, with **direction and drama**,
so it never gets boring. The plan to build it is
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-06-demo-composition-plan.md`, carried out by Claude Code
(on the show laptop, in VS Code) and Claude Desktop (in Ableton).

## What went wrong before, in a composer's words

- **No idea to follow.** The loops were patterns, not a theme. An audience holds on to a
  short, memorable melody that comes back changed; there was none.
- **No journey.** Instruments arrived one by one and then took turns. There was no tension
  and release: no hush before a return, no climb to a peak, no moment the room waits for.
- **The wrong sounds for the job.** Solo sampled trombone, clarinet and guitar, played by
  a sequencer, sound like a sequencer. Pads, a soft piano, glass, synth pulses and soft
  percussion sound like what they are and blend.
- **Pop harmony.** I V vi IV and its rotations (vi IV I V, and i VI III VII in minor are the
  same four chords) read as cheesy at once.

## Ambient first

Every sound, the loops' and Tim's counters' alike, follows these rules:

- **Soft in, long out.** No sound starts with a hard edge: slow attacks or soft mallets;
  every sound has a long tail and sits in the same large, shared space.
- **Nothing hits.** The rhythm is a pulse you feel, not a beat you hear: a soft heartbeat
  kick, brushed clicks, a shaker breath, toms like distant thunder, cymbals that swell rather
  than crash. The percussion stays under the pads at all times.
- **One scale for everything:** the F sharp minor pentatonic (F#, A, B, C#, E), and in the
  last section F sharp major pentatonic (F#, G#, A#, C#, D#). Every note of it sounds well
  over every chord of the piece, so **anything Tim places is consonant**, wherever he puts it.
- **Tim's counters become ambient voices** in the demo: a melody colour plays the soft lead
  or bells, a chord colour a soft pad, a bass colour the sub; long notes, in the pads' room.

## Never boring: what keeps changing

Ambient music fails when nothing moves. Here something changes at every level of time:

- **Every pass** (5.5 s): the theme asks on one pass and answers on the next; Magenta's
  sparkles fall in different places.
- **Every 4 bars** (11 s): the chord changes, slowly, with its colour notes.
- **Every scene** (22 to 44 s): one element arrives, leaves or is transformed (a new voice,
  the theme moving to another instrument or an octave up, the bass letting go of its pedal,
  the chords starting to move twice as often).
- **Within each scene:** the pads' own slow movement in Ableton (a filter or wavetable sweep
  over about 8 s), and Energy rising scene by scene, so the sound brightens as it climbs.
- **Across the piece:** a shape with drama: dark and close, a body arriving, a first climb,
  the hush, the long climb to the summit, then light. Drama comes from density, brightness,
  width and register (the theme rising an octave, the pads opening), not from loudness or
  drums.

## The idea

Cinematic ambient (think of Olafur Arnalds, Jon Hopkins, Sigur Ros, Brian Eno's warmer
records): a single four note theme born in the dark, carried by a pulse, rising to a summit
and resolving into light. Minor until the last minute, when it turns major (a Picardy
third): the payoff the whole piece has been waiting for.

- **Key:** F sharp minor pentatonic, the key note F#4 on the bottom row; the last section in
  F sharp major pentatonic.
- **Tempo:** 87 BPM; a pass is 2 bars (8 beats, about 5.5 s). It already lines the ending up
  with 7:00 exactly.
- **Length:** 7:00, ten scenes, ending with the Final chord (the texture thins, then the
  tonic chord, F sharp major, rings with a soft cymbal swell).

## The theme (the hook)

Four notes, a falling sigh and a reach upwards: **C#, B, A, then up to E.**

Written on the 8 steps of a pass. Degrees are steps of the pentatonic scale from the bottom
row: 0 = F#4, 1 = A4, 2 = B4, 3 = C#5, 4 = E5, 5 = F#5, 6 = A5, 7 = B5.

| Step | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question (every pass) | C# (3) held 3 beats | | | B (2) | A (1) held 2 beats | | E (4) held 2 beats | |
| Answer (every other pass, added) | | | | | | B (2) | | F#' (5) |

The first pass asks (C#, B, A, E, left open); the second answers (C#, B, A, B, E, F#,
falling back to C# as the next pass begins). In F sharp major pentatonic (degrees 0 = F#4,
1 = G#4, 2 = A#4, 3 = C#5, 4 = D#5, 5 = F#5) the same degrees sing C#, A#, G#, D#: the
theme's shape, in light.

The theme's journey: a distant fragment on glass bells (Dawn), whole on a soft felt piano
(Theme), carried over the pulse, alone in the hush, on a warm singing lead an octave up at
the summit, and finally in the major on the piano in the afterglow.

## Harmony: two progressions, no pop

Each chord is held 4 bars (2 passes) unless marked "moving" (2 bars). Pads voice every chord
with its colours (ninths, sevenths, suspensions), never as a plain triad.

- **Tide** (the night): i add9, VI maj7, iv m9, i add9. In F sharp minor: F#m(add9), Dmaj7,
  Bm9, F#m(add9). The minor plagal pull (iv to i) is dark and tender, not pop.
- **Rise** (the climb): VI maj7, VII sus4, i add9, i add9. Dmaj7, Esus4, F#m(add9). The
  Aeolian rise, VI VII i: the film climb, lifting to the home chord.
- **Drone**: F# and its fifth, for the dawn.
- **Float** in F sharp major, for the afterglow: F#(add9), then D#m(add9), each 4 bars.

The chords come from the key's parent scale (F sharp natural minor) while every written
note stays in the pentatonic: the pads carry D and G sharp; the tunes never rub against them.

The band's bass holds a **pedal F#** under Tide in the early scenes (the chords move above a
bass that does not), and follows the roots from Heartbeat on.

|---|---|---|---|

| Bells | melody, Bells (synth:bell), an octave up | Melody (1) | the theme's fragment: C# and A, held |
| Felt piano | melody, Piano | Melody (1) | the theme, question and answer |
| Soft lead | melody, Synth keys (an octave up at the summit) | Melody (1) | the theme, singing |
| Pulse | bass job, Synth pluck, an octave up, over the bass | Bass (2) | soft eighth notes on F# |
| Swells | chord, Soft synth pad, follows the pads, held a pass | Chords (3) | one swell a pass |
| Pulse chords | chord, Wide synth chords (softened), follows the pads | Chords (3) | soft rhythmic chords, 1, the and of 2, 4 |

Only one instrument per Ableton track sounds at a time, so each scene chooses one melody
sound, one Bass track sound and one Chords track sound (ADMI switches the track's chain on
the pass: loops play their own instrument; a counter of Tim's on the board takes its track).

### The pulse (eighth notes, written as two counters a step, the second half a beat late)

Degrees as above, an octave up from the bass register (so F#3 to B4):

| Beat | 1 | and | 2 | and | 3 | and | 4 | and |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Note | F# (0) | C# (3) | A (1) | E (4) | F# (0) | C# (3) | A (1) | B (2) |

The same for the second bar. On the strong beats it is always F#: a pedal that every chord
of Tide and Rise welcomes. The quarter note version for the afterglow plays degrees 0, 3, 1,
3 on beats 1 to 4 (F#, C#, G#, C# in the major).

### The percussion (half time, soft: the heartbeat)

- **Heartbeat**: a soft kick on 1 and on the and of 2; a rim click on 3 of each bar; a shaker
  breath on every "and" (eight a pass), very quiet.
- **Build**: as Heartbeat, plus a kick on 3 of bar 2, a soft brushed snare instead of the
  rim, the shaker on every eighth, and low toms rolling in on the last beat of every other
  pass.
- **Summit**: kick on 1, the and of 2, 3, the and of 4; the brushed snare on 3 of each bar;
  the shaker on every eighth; a cymbal swell on the first beat of every other pass.

## Magenta

The AI's extra notes are kept, and frozen. Magenta can't be told to play the same twice, so:
it improvises in a rehearsal, ADMI keeps each scene's ideas, the owner listens and keeps the
best, and those ideas are written into the demo. From then on every performance plays the
same AI ideas, the ones chosen by ear. (Today a kept idea is matched against everything on
the board, the player's colours included, so ideas kept with one player would not replay
for another: the plan changes that for loops alone.) Its notes are in the key's scale, so
they are as consonant as everything else.

## Tim's part, live

The demo plays by itself; Tim performs over it, and everything he does sounds ambient.

- **The cap, up and down: Tone** (brightness, the filter on every track in Ableton). From
  Heartbeat, Tim opens the sound as it climbs: the audience sees his head lift the music.
- **The cap, left and right: Extra notes** (how much Magenta adds). Or Speed, as now, if
  the owner prefers: then keep its range narrow (about 80 to 95 BPM), as scenes come in by
  the clock and a big speed change moves them off the fills.
- **His counters** play in the key's pentatonic scale through ambient voices (soft lead,
  bells, pad, sub): whatever he places sounds well; in the Hush they are the only thing
  besides the piano.
- **The iPad**: New idea (a fresh Magenta idea, his choice live), End piece.
