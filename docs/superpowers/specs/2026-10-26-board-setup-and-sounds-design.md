# Board Sequencer: an easier set-up, and choosing sounds in one table

Date: 2026-10-26 · Status: agreed in conversation, to build in stages and re-evaluate

## Why

The owner (a facilitator who sets ADMI up for Tim and other players) finds set-up overwhelming: "the process and user journey was cluttered and unintuitive". Choosing the sound for each colour is the worst part: "very unintuitive", with all the colours and scrolling. Two reviewers (a product designer and a hands-on tester, 2026-10-26) agreed: important steps are buried among things a facilitator doesn't need, sounds aren't part of set-up at all, and a colour's job and sound live on different screens.

How sounds are really chosen, in the owner's words: "I list the options and ask him and he chooses and then we listen and re-evaluate". They try "until it sounds right", and want "more choice".

Users: musicians with cerebral palsy, acquired brain injury or sensory impairments, and a facilitator. Tim is left-handed and can't dwell-hold or shake fast. Principles that matter here: tolerance over precision; musical agency; visual feedback for every sound; calibratable thresholds.

## Goals

1. Set-up is short and in a clear order, with nothing on screen a facilitator doesn't need.
2. Every colour can be set up from **one table**: one row per colour, all on one screen.
3. Choosing a sound is a quick loop: read the options out, Tim picks, hear it at once, go back if the last one was better.
4. The same table is in set-up and on the Play screen, and works while playing.
5. A returning session reaches the first sound in about two clicks.
6. A laptop board (no camera) has its own clear path.
7. More sounds to choose from.

## Non-goals

- Loops and performing (the next piece of work, group D).
- The Play screen's other tabs, row labels, the cap's defaults (group E).
- Ableton's own sounds: in Ableton mode the table still sets jobs; sounds there are Ableton's (as today).

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- Renaming "Melody" to anything like "Tune": the owner rejected it, because whether a colour plays single notes or phrases is a separate choice.
- A sound change that can't be heard until Play is pressed.
- Hiding what a colour does: the table must say it plainly for every colour.
- Losing a player's saved choices.

## The table (decided)

One row per colour found on the board:

| Colour | Plays | Sounds like | ▶ Hear | More |
|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Colour:** the swatch and its name.
- **Plays:** Melody, Bass, Chords, Drums, Not used. ("Chord stab" becomes "Chords" and "Off (not used)" becomes "Not used". Melody and Bass keep their names.)
- **Sounds like:** only sounds that suit what the colour plays, with short plain names that can be read aloud. Drums: the kit, or one drum piece.
- **▶ Hear:** plays a short phrase of that colour's sound at once, even with the board stopped. While playing, a change is heard on that colour's next notes.
- **Going back:** one tap returns that row to the sound it had before the last change (for comparing).
- **More** (per row, folded): one note or a phrase ("Each counter plays"), Loud / Bright / Space, Notes wander, Changes over time.
- The **Sound world** (Warm, Lo-fi, Ambient, Electronic) sits above the table as "fill in every colour at once"; any row can then be changed.
- Control colours (speed, volume, fill…) and loop colours are in a smaller section under the table, so the table is about sounds.
- What the player settles on is saved with their player, as now.

The table appears in two places: the Colours step of set-up, and the Play screen (replacing the per-colour cards on the Sound tab).

## The journey (decided)

**First time**
1. Who's playing? Name and hand.
2. Camera: picks the last-used or external camera itself; only speaks up about a problem (no picture, black and white, too dark).
3. Board: "Put the board in view, empty" → Find board → Looks right. The empty board is learnt as part of this, automatically ("✓ Board found and learnt"). Rows, steps, sizes and the testing tools move under "More".
4. Colours: "Put one of each counter down" → Find colours → the table. Set what each colour plays and how it sounds, with ▶ Hear.
5. ▶ Play.

**Returning session:** Play my board → straight to the Play screen, showing "✓ Board found" → ▶ Play. If the board seems to have moved, the screen says so and offers Find board.

**No camera:** a clear "Use the laptop board" choice on the welcome screen, and a set-up that skips the camera and board steps (colours and the table only).

## More sounds (the builder's call, starting point)

Already bundled but not offered: violin, upright bass (contrabass), tuba, choir pad. Synth sounds that today exist only inside the worlds (synth pluck, bells, keys, soft pad, wide saw chords, saw bass, sub bass) can be offered by name too. More sampled instruments from the same free library (nbrosowsky/tonejs-instruments, CC-BY 3.0: flute, saxophone, trumpet, trombone, bassoon, xylophone, acoustic and electric guitar) can follow as a separate step, with attribution.

## Calls made after the builder check (my calls, for the owner to check)

1. **A Sound world never overwrites a row's own choice.** As today, a colour with its own pick keeps it; the others follow the world. "Sounds like" shows "Warm world's sound (Piano + soft pad)" as its first option, so a row following the world reads plainly. A "Reset every colour to the world" action sits beside the world choice. "My picks" (no world) stays; the world still sets the drums' tone, the room and the swing.
2. **Every colour is a row.** "Plays" also offers Control (with which one: Speed, Volume, Extra notes…) and Loop (which loop). Those rows show "—" under "Sounds like" and are grouped after the sound rows, so the table stays about sounds without hiding any colour.
3. **In Ableton mode** "Sounds like" shows Ableton's sound for that colour (as today), and ▶ Hear sends a short preview phrase to Ableton on that colour's channel (the test-note path), so it is heard where the audience hears it.
4. **Choosing the player on a returning session** is decided in stage 2, after stage 1 has been tried.

## Left to the builder

- The exact sound list per job, its order and grouping, and the names.
- How ▶ Hear is made (a short phrase in the colour's own register, quantised so it is musical, visual flash for deaf and hard-of-hearing users).
- How "going back" is stored (per row, last one only is enough).
- Whether a job change while playing is applied live or briefly stops the music (it must not send the facilitator back to set-up).
- Layout details: tap targets large enough for a facilitator on a laptop; keyboard and screen-reader access.

## Stages (each one usable, then re-evaluated with the owner)

1. The colour table on the Play screen and in set-up's Colours step, with ▶ Hear and going back; the new sounds that are already bundled.
2. The shorter journey: returning sessions straight to Play; the board learnt automatically; testing tools and sizes under "More"; the camera step quieter.
3. The laptop-board path.
4. More sampled instruments (download, attribution).
